# Director Introduction Video — Word-for-Word Production Script

**Murivest Private Capital Portal · Director's Trust Introduction**

Version: 1.0 · Prepared for: the Director, Murivest Group Ltd · Runtime target: ~4 minutes 15 seconds (~610 spoken words at 145 wpm)

This document is the complete production package: what to say (word for word), how to say it, what appears on screen while you say it, and why every beat exists from a persuasion standpoint (Ogilvy discipline, NEPQ tone). Items in `[SQUARE BRACKETS]` are the only placeholders — fill them before recording.

---

## 1. Purpose and strategic brief

| Item | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Primary audience | UHNWIs, family offices, institutional representatives — people who could allocate to a large global manager and are considering direct allocation instead |
| Job of the video | Convert *institutional scepticism* into *specific trust*: the viewer should finish thinking "this person shows the numbers, refuses to over-promise, and the product answers my six questions" |
| Tone | Low-key authority. Consultative, never salesy. You are a counterparty introducing yourself, not a presenter pitching |
| One sentence the viewer should remember | "See the underwriting before you write the cheque." |
| Placement | Landing page, embedded in "Meet the Director" section, directly beneath the Director's letter; also embedded in the investor manual (QR/link) |
| Success metric | Request-access completions within 7 days of watching; manual downloads from the same session |

**Why a video at all (not just copy):** at eight-to-nine-figure decision levels, the binding constraint is *counterparty risk* — "is this firm real, is this person real, will they behave when things get difficult." Headlines cannot answer that; a face, a voice, a real office and an honest set of refusals can. This video is the single highest-leverage trust artefact on the page.

## 2. Production checklist (before the camera rolls)

**Location** — your office at 4th Avenue Parklands, Diamond Plaza, Nairobi (primary), with one London-desk shot if available. Depth behind you matters: bookshelf, property plans on the wall, or the window line — anything that says *working office*, not *studio*.

**Wardrobe** — what a client would meet you in. Plain, dark tones sit best on camera; avoid fine stripes and checks (they strobe). No novelty items. If in doubt, wear what you wore to your last serious client meeting.

**Camera** — 4K if available (allows reframing for social cutdowns), at eye level, ~1.8–2 m away. Framing: mid-shot, eyes on the upper third, a little room above the head. Lock exposure and focus manually before the first take.

**Audio** — a lapel mic or a shotgun within 60 cm. Record 30 seconds of room silence first (backgrounds removal for noise reduction). Phones on silent; HVAC off if it hums.

**Lighting** — key light 30–45° to one side at a gentle downward angle; window light is acceptable if it is soft and not behind you. Never sit with a bright window behind you.

**Teleprompter** — place the script page-level just under the lens line. Practise the segment aloud **twice before recording** — the aim is to sound like you are *remembering* the thought, not reading it. If a take feels read, slow down 10% and try again.

**B-roll list (30–60 s clips, shot the same day):**

1. Office exterior / Diamond Plaza approach shot
2. You at a desk with a printed underwriting pack (hands visible, faces blurred)
3. Screen recording: the live portal — landing page scroll, Discover (empty state is fine), Investment Studio controls, Orders statuses
4. London desk or a recognizable London establishing shot (if a second location is possible)
5. A handshake / meeting-room empty-then-occupied beat (for the mandate close)

**Never do in the edit:** stock footage of "money" or "handshakes you did not attend", stock skylines of cities you do not operate in, energetic background music, whoosh transitions, countdown timers. Every one of those signals the opposite of private banking.

## 3. Compliance guardrails (read before recording — non-negotiable)

1. **No performance promises.** Do not say "guaranteed", "assured", "risk-free", "you will make", or name any historical return figure of a specific asset unless it is in a published, document-backed brief. If a sentence could be replayed in a regulator's office, it must still be true and fair.
2. **No fabricated social proof.** No invented client counts, no "hundreds of family offices", no testimonials. Real numbers only — and only if you can evidence them in writing.
3. **The structural statements are facts, keep them exact.** "We do not pool capital", "we do not operate collective investment schemes", "mandate-only", "KYC/AML verified", "fiduciary standards" — these reflect the firm's actual operating basis; do not improvise variants of them.
4. **No disparagement of named competitors or asset classes.** The shared enemy is *opacity*, not any firm. Keep it that way.
5. **No urgency mechanics.** No countdowns, no "only today", no scarcity of access. The product's actual exclusivity (verification-gated, off-market) is the only scarcity you may reference, stated as fact.
6. If ad-libbing, ad-lib **toward specificity and toward refusals** — never toward promises.

## 4. The script — time-coded, word-for-word

> Reading conventions: **SPEECH** = exact words to say (teleprompter copy is §7). **DELIVERY** = how to say it. **ON SCREEN** = lower thirds / titles. **B-ROLL** = cutaway. **WHY IT WORKS** = the persuasion logic of the beat.

---

### SEGMENT 1 · The hook (0:00 – 0:22)

**SPEECH**

> "If you manage serious capital, there is one sentence you have probably heard more than once from a fund manager: *'the underwriting is proprietary.'* This video exists to do the exact opposite of that sentence."

**DELIVERY** — open standing or seated; still; no smile yet; land the italicised line slowly and slightly quieter than the sentence before it. The contrast in volume *is* the hook.

**ON SCREEN** — none for the first 8 seconds (face only), then a restrained lower third:

> [YOUR NAME] · Director, Murivest Group Ltd · Nairobi

**B-ROLL** — none. Direct-to-camera throughout.

**WHY IT WORKS** — *Curiosity/information gap + incongruity.* The viewer instantly recognises the shared experience (the withheld underwriting), and the promise of its reversal creates an open loop that only watching can close. This is the Ogilvy headline function, spoken.

---

### SEGMENT 2 · Who I am (0:22 – 0:58)

**SPEECH**

> "I'm [YOUR NAME], Director of Murivest Group Ltd. We are an international commercial real estate services and investment firm, working from Nairobi and London, and we do one specific thing: we connect institutional investors, family offices and sophisticated private investors to Grade A, income-producing commercial assets across East Africa and the United Kingdom — directly, on a mandate-only basis, under fiduciary standards."

**DELIVERY** — even pace; a small genuine smile only on "Nairobi and London". Slight emphasis on "directly", "mandate-only", "fiduciary standards" — the three load-bearing words of the whole film.

**ON SCREEN** —

> Murivest Group Ltd · Commercial real estate services & investment
> Nairobi · London · Grade A income-producing assets

**B-ROLL** — exterior/approach shot of the office; a London establishing beat on "London".

**WHY IT WORKS** — *Authority + positioning + liking.* Facts, verifiable specifics, zero adjectives. Institutional viewers trust nouns and numbers; they distrust superlatives. The three emphasised words are the exact differentiators against pooled alternatives.

---

### SEGMENT 3 · The problem, as you have seen it (0:58 – 1:38)

**SPEECH**

> "Over the years I have sat on both sides of that table. And I have watched serious capital go into structures where nobody — including the investor — could see the assumptions underneath: the vacancy case, the exit maths, the leverage. Exposure without control. A projection you can't interrogate. And a fee schedule you pay either way. Now — that system genuinely works for many people. But if you have ever left one of those meetings asking yourself *'what exactly am I buying?'* — that question is the reason this portal exists."

**DELIVERY** — this is the empathy beat. Slow down ~10%. Hands quiet. Let the list of three ("Exposure without control…") land with a beat between items. On the italicised question, look directly at the lens and pause two full seconds before moving on.

**ON SCREEN** — none; keep the human face. Optional: at "…the exit maths, the leverage", a single subtle white-text overlay:

> What exactly am I buying?

**B-ROLL** — none for the question itself; optionally a 2-second shot of a printed underwriting pack on the desk before the segment.

**WHY IT WORKS** — *NEPQ situation → problem-awareness.* The narration mirrors the viewer's own inner conversation instead of pushing a claim — this is what makes NEPQ feel like consultation rather than pitching. *Validation* ("that system genuinely works for many people") pre-empts the viewer's defences: you are not attacking their past choices, you are articulating their doubt. *Shared enemy = opacity*, never a named firm.

### SEGMENT 4 · What we built — the six answers (1:38 – 2:55)

**SPEECH**

> "So we built the Murivest Private Capital Portal around six things a serious principal should never have to ask twice.
>
> One — *Discover.* Off-market, auction-sourced and marketed Grade A opportunities, filtered to your verified access tier. Not a public listing feed.
>
> Two — *underwriting you can actually see.* Net operating income, yields, lease profiles, stress cases, downside scenarios — in writing, on request.
>
> Three — the *Investment Studio.* Model the capital structure yourself: leverage, hold period, exit. And when your equity is short of the asking price, the capital-gap engine maps financing pathways instead of dismissing the deal.
>
> Four — *Orders.* Deep institutional briefs with explicit scope and pricing, ordered and paid in minutes — M-Pesa, wire or card — and tracked from pending, to in progress, to delivered.
>
> Five — *one secure workspace.* Documents, mandates, decisions, audit trail — watermarked, logged, in one place.
>
> Six — *privacy.* Graduated, verified access. No noise. No marketing churn. Ever."

**DELIVERY** — energy rises one notch here; this is the "tour" and it earns it. Count the six on your fingers only if that is natural to you — do not force it. Punch the six italicised module names slightly. Keep each item to one breath group; the teleprompter copy (§7) is line-broken for exactly this.

**ON SCREEN** — a restrained white-text overlay per item, appearing as you speak it, e.g.:

> 01 Discover — off-market · auction-sourced · tier-gated
> 02 Underwriting — NOI · yields · stress cases
> 03 Investment Studio — leverage · hold · exit · capital gap
> 04 Orders — scope · price · M-Pesa / wire / card
> 05 Workspace — documents · mandates · audit
> 06 Privacy — graduated · verified · quiet

**B-ROLL** — this is where the screen recordings go: live portal footage synced to each item (Discover list, Studio controls, Orders status chip row). Blur or crop any real investor data; the portal's genuine empty states are acceptable and honest.

**WHY IT WORKS** — *Future pacing + specificity.* The viewer mentally inhabits the product before registering. Every line is a concrete capability, not a benefit adjective — Ogilvy's "give them facts" discipline. Item three quietly contains the product's most distinctive promise (never reject an opportunity just because equity < price), stated as behaviour, not brag.

---

### SEGMENT 5 · What we are not (2:55 – 3:30)

**SPEECH**

> "Now — what we are *not*, because you deserve that on camera, not in the fine print. We are not a fund. We do not pool capital. We do not operate collective investment schemes. We do not sell unlicensed financial products. If anyone on my team ever tells you a number is 'proprietary' — you have my direct line. And I will not promise you a return; nobody honest can. What I can put in front of you is the underwriting, the stress cases, and the fees — before you write any cheque."

**DELIVERY** — the trust beat. Drop the energy back down; this must feel like the most deliberate 35 seconds of the film. Slight head-shake on "not a fund". The direct-line sentence should be said with a small, confident half-smile — it is the one permitted moment of personality.

**ON SCREEN** — a single static card behind you (dark green, white type), reading:

> Not a fund · No pooled capital · No collective schemes
> Mandate-only · Fiduciary standards · KYC/AML verified

**B-ROLL** — none. Face, steady.

**WHY IT WORKS** — *"Making your skeletons dance."* Volunteering the disqualifications before the viewer raises them is the single most disarming move available to a private-capital pitch — it reframes every unsaid objection as evidence of integrity. Note the structure: refusals → accountability line → a *refusal to promise returns* (which paradoxically raises credibility) → then the promise you *can* make (process transparency).

---

### SEGMENT 6 · The safeguards (3:30 – 3:52)

**SPEECH**

> "Everything inside the portal runs the way an institutional counterparty should expect: KYC and AML verification, a confidentiality agreement before any confidential material, watermarked documents with access logs, and sessions you can revoke per device. That friction is not a sales tactic. It protects both sides of an off-market transaction."

**DELIVERY** — brisk and matter-of-fact; safeguards stated as plumbing, not drama.

**ON SCREEN** —

> KYC/AML · NDA-gated data room · Watermarked documents · Per-device sessions

**B-ROLL** — optional 2-second shot of the security page of the portal (session list).

**WHY IT WORKS** — *Security as status.* For the target viewer, strict verification is not an obstacle — it is a marker that the other parties in the room are equally vetted. Reframing friction as protection converts the portal's biggest practical cost into a benefit.

---

### SEGMENT 7 · The close — and the power to say no (3:52 – 4:15)

**SPEECH**

> "Here is how I would suggest you start. Download the investor manual on this page — it walks through every module of the portal, step by step, before you share anything with us. Then request access, complete verification, and judge us on the first thing we show you. If it is not up to your standard — walk away, and keep the manual with my compliments. Where you go from here is entirely your decision. That is rather the point. I am [YOUR NAME], Director of Murivest Group Ltd. I look forward to earning your mandate."

**DELIVERY** — the warmest read of the film; a full genuine smile at "keep the manual with my compliments", then settle. Final sentence unhurried — it is the last thing they hear.

**ON SCREEN** — end card, dark green, gold rule, white type:

> MURIVEST · Private Capital Portal
> Request private access
> Investor manual (PDF) · [portal URL]
> [PHONE] · office@murivest.com

Hold the end card 4 seconds. No music sting.

**B-ROLL** — optional: the meeting-room handshake beat under the end card.

**WHY IT WORKS** — *NEPQ directive close + the power to say no + reciprocity.* The manual is a genuine gift that requires nothing — reciprocity without obligation. Explicitly granting the exit ("walk away") removes the pressure whose presence is the #1 trigger of institutional scepticism, which paradoxically makes saying yes easier. The last sentence of a video is its headline — "earning your mandate" is the whole positioning in five words.

## 5. Delivery coaching — the five rules

1. **Pace.** Target ~145 words per minute. Institutional trust reads as *slower*. When unsure, slow down; never speed up.
2. **Notes, not a script, in your head.** Know the *thought* of each segment, not the exact words — the prompter supplies words; your face must supply thinking.
3. **Eyes.** Lens-level, 90%+ of runtime. Blink naturally; look down only at natural segment boundaries.
4. **Hands.** Quiet by default; one gesture max per segment, below frame-line, open palms.
5. **The two-second pauses** in segments 1, 3 and 7 are part of the script. Silence, at this level, is a trust signal — do not fill it.

## 6. Post-production and publishing notes

- **Edit rhythm:** single-camera direct address for all seven segments; B-roll only as specified. Hard cuts or 6-frame dissolves — no transitions with personality.
- **Audio:** normalize to −16 LUFS integrated, true peak −1 dB. Remove room tone; add no music bed under speech. (Optional: a barely-audible room-tone bed under the end card only.)
- **Captions:** burn in open captions or attach a proper `.vtt`/`.srt` (also feed it to the portal when the video is embedded — accessibility and GEO both benefit). The full transcript is §7 of this document.
- **File & hosting:** host on your CDN or the Sanity media library as a single MP4 (H.264, 1080p, ≤ 30 MB if possible). Configure the landing page through Sanity → *Landing Page Content* → **Director introduction video URL** (fields: video URL, poster frame, your name, caption line) — or set `NEXT_PUBLIC_DIRECTOR_VIDEO_URL`, `NEXT_PUBLIC_DIRECTOR_VIDEO_POSTER`, `NEXT_PUBLIC_DIRECTOR_NAME` in the app environment. Until then the landing page shows an honest "being published" state with this script downloadable — never a placeholder video.
- **Poster frame:** a still of you mid-sentence at ~40% into segment 4 — expression engaged, not posed. Avoid the folded-arms cliché.
- **Suggested filenames:** `murivest-director-introduction.mp4`, `murivest-director-introduction-poster.jpg`, `murivest-director-introduction.vtt`.
