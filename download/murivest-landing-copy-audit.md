# Landing Page Copy Audit — Ogilvy · NEPQ · Weapons of Influence

**Murivest Private Capital Portal (`/`) · Sales-Letter Landing Page · Audit v1.0**

---

## 1. Scope and method

The landing page was audited and rebuilt against three persuasion systems, at the standard appropriate to an eight-to-nine-figure financial buyer:

1. **Ogilvy discipline** (direct-response advertising): headline as 80 cents of the dollar; facts over adjectives; long copy for considered purchases; give the reader something to do; subheads for the skimming majority; the P.S. as the second-most-read element; raise objections honestly.
2. **NEPQ (Neuro-Emotional Persuasion Questions)**: situation → problem-awareness → consequence → solution framed as partner-not-pitcher; detached, consultative tone; the prospect's own inner conversation mirrored back to them; low-pressure directive close with explicit permission to say no.
3. **The 37 weapons of mass influence** (per the Murivest masterclass handout): each weapon mapped to where it is used — and which are deliberately excluded in a fiduciary, regulated context.

## 2. Verdict on the previous version

The pre-audit page was a competent *institutional brochure*, not a *sales letter*. Specific findings:

| # | Finding | Why it fell short of standard | Fix applied |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Headline "Allocate private capital with institutional discipline." — a positioning slogan, not a sale | No curiosity gap, no tension, no reader-specific promise; it describes the firm rather than arrest the reader | New headline: **"See the underwriting before you write the cheque."** — 9 words, benefit + specificity + an implicit challenge to every pooled pitch the reader has ever sat through |
| 2 | No letter, no narrative arc — sections of equal weight, brochure cadence | Ogilvy: the reader is sold by a *sequence*; NEPQ lives in a conversation, not a feature grid | A **five-minute letter from the Director** now sits second on the page, carrying the full NEPQ arc (see §4) |
| 3 | No trust anchor — no face, no voice, no signature | Counterparty risk is the binding constraint at this ticket size; copy alone cannot retire it | **Director's introduction video block** (with word-for-word script downloadable) directly under the letter, plus the letter's hand-signed close |
| 4 | Objections never raised | Unspoken objections stay unspoken and poison the CTA | A dedicated **"Questions we would ask if we were you"** objection section (6 items) + FAQPage JSON-LD for search/GEO |
| 5 | No risk reversal, no exit | High-trust buyers escalate commitment when the exit is visibly safe | **P.S. block**: take the manual first, "walk away and keep the manual with my compliments" — reciprocity + power-to-say-no |
| 6 | CTA present but single-register | Ogilvy's "sequence of i-dots": many small yeses through the letter | CTAs now appear at 5 altitudes: hero, letter close, video rail, standard section, final CTA + P.S. |
| 7 | Value props were feature-shaped ("off-market opportunity intelligence") | Features inform; only promises sell | Recast as **the six questions a serious principal asks** — each card is a question the reader has actually asked, followed by the module that answers it |
| 8 | No downloadable artefact of substance | Reciprocity at this level requires a real gift, not a newsletter | **Investor manual (PDF)** and **Director's video script (MD)** downloadable without a form — no email gate, stated explicitly |

## 4. NEPQ mapping of the letter (the spine of the page)

| NEPQ level | Letter text | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Situation (connect) | "If you already invest through large managers, you know the ritual…" | Mirrors the prospect's lived experience; zero pressure; FK reading grade low |
| Problem-awareness (engage) | "…let me ask the question our best clients asked… what exactly are you buying?" | The question is the prospect's own, returned to them |
| Consequence (assess) | "Exposure to a market — but not control of an asset… a fee schedule you pay either way." | Three-beat consequence chain, no fear-mongering, no rival named |
| Transition (solution-as-partner) | "We built Murivest differently… we do not pool capital…" | Positioning by facts and refusals; no superlatives |
| Directive close (objective) | "…if the standard matches yours — or the standard you want to hold — request access. Where you go from there is entirely your decision." | Explicit permission to decline; decision ownership returned to the reader |

## 5. The 37 weapons — usage matrix

**Deployed (and where):**

| Weapon | Where used |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Curiosity / info gaps | Headline ("before you write the cheque" — an open loop); "what exactly are you buying?" |
| 2. Storytelling (hero's journey lite) | The letter's ritual→question→refusal→offer arc |
| 3. Conversational language (low FK) | Letter and FAQ written for the ear: short sentences, second person, no jargon |
| 5. "It's not your fault" (validation) | "That system genuinely works for many people…" — past choices validated before the alternative is offered |
| 6. Identity | "To a principal managing serious capital" — the reader is named into an identity in line one |
| 11. Reciprocity | The manual and script downloadable ungated; "keep the manual with my compliments" |
| 12. Authority | Director's signature and video; fiduciary/KYC facts; offices named |
| 14. Commitment & consistency (light) | Multi-altitude CTAs; the letter's "watch it, download the manual, take your time" micro-commitment ladder |
| 15. Social proof (structural only) | Real coverage facts (Nairobi/London, auction site) — deliberately **no invented testimonials or client counts** |
| 16. Metaphor | "The quietest room your capital occupies"; "your side of the table"; "the velvet rope" |
| 18. Predictions (hedged) | "Markets reward investors who can price risk" — principle stated, no market forecast made |
| 19. Power to say no | "Walk away and keep the manual"; "Where you go from here is entirely your decision" |
| 24. Commanding language (restrained) | Imperatives only where they protect the reader: "Read this before you request access" |
| 25. Future pacing | The six-answers section: mentally operating the portal before registering |
| 26. Positioning | The whole page positions Murivest as the *direct, non-pooled alternative* to pooled allocations |
| 28. Objection raising | FAQ section; "What Murivest is not" section |
| 29. Exclusivity (velvet rope) | Graduated access section — genuine, structural, honestly priced |
| 30. Simplicity | One idea per section; a single primary CTA per altitude |
| 32. Empathy | The letter's problem beats; "questions we would ask if we were you" |
| 35. Gradualization | Onboarding is presented as five gentle steps, not a leap |
| 36. Transparency ("making your skeletons dance") | "What Murivest is not"; the video script's on-camera refusals; honest empty states |

**Deliberately excluded (with reasons — this is a fiduciary, regulated context, not a consumer funnel):**

| Weapon | Excluded because |
| --- | --- |
| 7. The 7 deadly sins (lust/gluttony/greed/sloth baiting) | Baiting vice in financial services is regulator-facing territory and corrodes exactly the audience this product targets |
| 10. Scarcity (manufactured) | Off-market exclusivity is stated as *fact* (it is structural); countdown timers / "last seats" framings would be fabricated urgency and are never used |
| 21. Mysticism | "Manifesting returns" language is the antithesis of institutional underwriting |
| 27. Gaslighting | Manipulating a counterparty's reality in a fiduciary relationship is not persuasion, it is misconduct |
| 33. FOMO | Manufactured FOMO contradicts "take your time" — the page's core trust posture |
| 34. Conspiracy theories | "They don't want you to see the numbers" is only ever used as a *true structural observation* about pooled products, never as conspiratorial framing; no villain is named |
| 37. "Declare war" on rivals | No named competitor is attacked; the shared enemy is opacity, never a firm |

Remaining weapons (4 shared enemy, 8 anticipation, 13 liking, 17 incongruity, 20 power words, 22–23 total certainty, 31 quirky language) are used at trace level only where they do not compromise the register: incongruity in the headline's tension, anticipation in "Watch the video below", power words confined to factual ones (underwriting, mandate, fiduciary, audit). **"Total certainty" is banned from performance claims by design** — the only certainty permitted is about process.

## 6. Ogilvy checklist

- [x] Headline promises a reader-specific benefit with specificity (9 words, no adjectives)
- [x] First paragraph of the letter hooks with a recognised ritual, not a boast
- [x] Long copy — the page is a full letter, on the direct-response logic that consideration time at this ticket size is paid for by depth
- [x] Facts, figures, names — offices, sectors, coverage, payment rails, module names; **no invented statistics or testimonials**
- [x] Subheads and skimmable blocks for the 80% who skim (eyebrows, cards, numbered items)
- [x] Multiple "i-dots": five CTA altitudes from hero to P.S.
- [x] Objections raised honestly by the seller (FAQ + "what we are not")
- [x] P.S. present and doing work (the ungated manual offer)
- [x] No braggadocio; no superlatives; the consumer is treated as intelligent (Ogilvy: "The consumer isn't a moron; she is your wife" — here, she is a principal)
- [x] Coupon-equivalent present: the manual download with no email capture

## 7. Compliance notes carried by the copy

1. No return figures, no guarantees, no "assured" anything — the only promises are about process transparency and fee documentation.
2. The pooled-fund contrast never names or caricatures a rival; it attacks opacity, and concedes "that system genuinely works for many people."
3. Structural claims ("we do not pool capital", "not a collective investment scheme", "mandate-only", "KYC/AML") match the operating basis of Murivest Group Ltd as provided in the company brief — they are facts, not positioning flourishes.
4. FAQPage JSON-LD mirrors visible answers only (no dark-pattern schema); Organization JSON-LD carries the real address, phone and coverage.
